Why this comes first
The next steps will clean up and erase things on this computer — which also erases clues about how this happened. Before you touch the computer, use your phone to grab a few screenshots so Ari can try to figure out how they got in. This takes 2 minutes and is safe to do.
🔑
Look through 1Password on your phone
Open 1Password like you normally would, then tap "All Items" to see your full list of saved logins.

Slowly scroll down through the list, just reading the names. If anything looks off — a website you don't remember using, an account name you don't recognize, or the same site listed twice — that's what we're looking for. Take a screenshot of the list so it shows.

To screenshot on your Pixel: press the Power button and the Volume Down button at the same time, then let go. It saves itself to your Photos app — nothing else to do.

Not sure if something counts as "off"? Screenshot it anyway — it's much easier for Ari to say "that one's fine" than to guess what you saw.
🗑️
Check your email's Trash / Deleted folder
Open your email app on your phone. Tap the Trash or Deleted folder. If you see emails there that you don't remember deleting — especially ones from your bank, Google, or 1Password — take a screenshot of the list.
📤
Check your email's Sent folder
Now tap your Sent folder. If there are any emails you don't remember sending, take a screenshot of those too.
📩
Send the screenshots to Ari, whenever is convenient
Text or email whatever screenshots you took to Ari — there's no rush, and no need to wait for a reply before moving on to the next step below. Send everything you screenshotted, even if none of it looks important to you. Ari will review asap and sort out what matters for investigation and documenting proof, if needed.

A few safety check-ups, still on your phone
Still your phone, no rush
🛋️
Why this helps
Think of this like changing the locks, just to be safe — a few quick check-ups on your phone so your accounts can only be reached by you. None of this is urgent, and there's nothing here you can get wrong. Take your time.
🚪
Sign out of other devices on your email account
1. On your phone, open a web browser and go to myaccount.google.com (or the equivalent security page for whichever email you use).

2. Tap "Security", then look for something like "Your devices" and tap "Manage all devices".

3. You'll see a list of things signed into your account. This is just a tidy-up — if you see a device you don't recognize, tap it and choose "Sign out". If everything looks familiar, that's great too — nothing more to do here.
🔄
Give your email password a quick refresh
While you're in the same Security settings, tap "Password" and set a new one you haven't used before. If you see an option called "2-Step Verification" and it isn't already turned on, turning it on adds a second, easy lock — it'll text a code to your phone whenever anyone tries to sign in.
🔑
Peek at 1Password's device list
On your phone, open a browser and go to 1password.com, then sign in. Look for a "Security" or "Active Sessions" section — it shows which devices are signed into your account. Same as before: anything unfamiliar, sign it out; if it all looks like your own devices, you're done here.
🏦
Optional: a glance at your bank app
If anything in your earlier screenshots looked bank-related, it's worth opening your banking app on your phone and skimming your recent activity. Only if something looks unfamiliar is there anything to act on — otherwise, just a quick look and move on.

⬇ Download Cleanup Tool

Open the downloaded file
Find the file called CleanupTool.zip and right-click on it. Choose "Extract All..." from the menu that appears, then click the "Extract" button on the next window.
This creates a new folder called "CleanupTool" with two files inside.
🖱️
Double-click "Run-Cleanup"
Open the new CleanupTool folder and double-click the file named Run-Cleanup.

A blue box will pop up asking for permission — click "Yes".

A black window will open with white text scrolling by. This is normal — leave it alone and let it run. It will take a few minutes.

When it says "ALL DONE!", you can close the window.

Open Malwarebytes and remove threats
Open Malwarebytes from your desktop or Start menu. If it shows a list of items found, make sure they are all checked, then click the button labeled "Quarantine". If it asks to restart the computer, click "Yes, restart now."
🧹
Run AdwCleaner
Open your Desktop, then the CleanupTools folder. Double-click AdwCleaner. Click "Scan Now." When it finishes, click "Quarantine."
🔍
Run HitmanPro
In the same CleanupTools folder, double-click HitmanPro. Click "Next" to start scanning. When it's done, click "Next" again to remove anything it finds.

Install Chrome again
Open the blue Edge browser (it's already on your computer). Type google.com/chrome in the address bar at the top and press Enter. Click the blue "Download Chrome" button, then open the downloaded file and click through the installer.
⭐
Bring your bookmarks back
Open the new Chrome. Click the three dots in the top-right corner → Bookmarks → Import Bookmarks and Settings. Choose "Bookmarks HTML file" and select the file on your Desktop inside the CleanupTools folder called bookmarks_backup.
