2️⃣

Open the cleanup.ps1 file attached to this guide on your own machine, then either:

Option A — Copy/paste the full script content directly into the PowerShell window on her machine.

Option B — If file transfer is available via your remote tool, copy cleanup.ps1 to her Desktop and run:
& "$env:USERPROFILE\Desktop\cleanup.ps1"


The script will automatically: quarantine known bad startup keys · uninstall Chrome + delete its AppData · set DNS to Quad9 · download AdwCleaner and HitmanPro to her Desktop.

Your machine
3️⃣
Reset her Google Sync to wipe the malicious extensions from the cloud
On your own computer, log into her Google account, then visit:
https://myaccount.google.com/data-and-privacy
Navigate to Manage your data & privacy → Delete a Google service, or go directly to Chrome sync settings and click "Reset sync" / "Clear data from Google". This prevents the malicious extensions from re-syncing to the clean Chrome install.
C
Manual scans on her machine (you walk her through, or do via remote)
Manual — remote
4️⃣
Malwarebytes — quarantine all 45 items
Open Malwarebytes. Make sure all detected items are checked. Click Quarantine. When prompted to restart, click Yes.
Note: Restarting will briefly drop your remote session. Reconnect after ~2 minutes.
5️⃣
Run AdwCleaner (already downloaded to her Desktop\CleanupTools)
Double-click AdwCleaner.exe. Click Scan Now. When complete, click Quarantine on everything found. Specifically targets browser policy hijackers like OneLaunch and Adware.Elex that Malwarebytes may have missed.
6️⃣
Run HitmanPro (already downloaded to her Desktop\CleanupTools)
Double-click HitmanPro.exe. Choose No, I only want to perform a one-time scan. Let it run — it uses cloud engines (Sophos, Kaspersky, Bitdefender) to catch rootkits that local scanners miss. Quarantine anything found. Free trial is fine.
7️⃣
Check Task Manager startup list manually
Press Ctrl + Shift + Esc → Startup apps tab. The PowerShell script removed known bad entries by name, but scroll through visually. Disable anything you don't recognize — especially items with no publisher, blank icons, or suspicious paths like AppData\Local\Temp.

Reinstall Chrome, restore bookmarks + safe extensions
Quick steps
8️⃣
Download and reinstall Chrome using Edge
Open Microsoft Edge on her machine. Navigate to:
https://www.google.com/chrome/
Download and install. Do not sign into her Google account yet — wait until you've confirmed the sync reset (Step 3) has processed.
9️⃣
Import her bookmarks back into new Chrome
The script saved bookmarks_backup.html to her Desktop\CleanupTools folder before wiping Chrome. To restore them:

In the new Chrome → press Ctrl+Shift+O to open Bookmarks → click the ⋮ three-dot menu → Import bookmarks → navigate to Desktop\CleanupTools\bookmarks_backup.html and open it.

Her bookmarks will appear in a new "Imported" folder in the bookmarks bar.
🔟
Review extensions_report.html before reinstalling anything
Open Desktop\CleanupTools\extensions_report.html in Edge. The script read every extension from her infected profile and tagged each one:

Known safe — these can be reinstalled from the Chrome Web Store without concern.
Review before reinstalling — click the ID link to open its Web Store page. Check the name matches, verify the developer, and check reviews. If anything looks off, skip it.
Built-in — skip — Chrome internals, not user-installed, ignore these.

Do not bulk-reinstall. Add them back one at a time so if something breaks, you know exactly what caused it.
1️⃣1️⃣
Install uBlock Origin Lite first (before anything else)
In the new Chrome, go to:
https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/ublock-origin-lite/ddkjiahejlhfcafbddmgiahcphecmpfh
Install this before any other extensions and before any general browsing. It blocks the fake "Download Here" buttons that likely caused this infection.
